  • Date: 21st October 2026
    Time: 3-4pm
    Location: B20 Jevons Lecture Theatre, Drayton House
    Speaker: Andreea Balmus (UCL)
    Title: Biological networks in drug-microbiome interactions
    Abstract: he current machine learning models for predicting drug-microbiome interactions are limited in scope, as they can only estimate a xenobiotic’s effect on a single bacterial species in vitro. As a result, they fail to capture the following complex interactions between multiple bacterial species, and so cannot predict how a xenobiotic (pharmaceutical drug) will ultimately change the composition of the human gut microbiome.
    This talk will explore extending the current models by incorporating biological networks for inter-species microbial interactions. A Random Forest will first predict the direct effect of a xenobiotic on each bacterial species. These predictions will then serve as inputs to a Bayesian Network that encodes the ecological relationships between species, such as parasitism, mutualism, and commensalism. Given a starting gut microbiome composition and the chemical properties of the xenobiotic, can we predict the microbiome’s final composition?
