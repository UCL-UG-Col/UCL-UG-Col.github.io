  • Date: 11th November 2026
    Talking History of Maths
    Location: online (because it's reading week)
    Register for the online talk using the following form: https://forms.cloud.microsoft/e/mncqRHTQzz
    Time: 2-3.30pm

    Speaker: Dr Michael Barany
    Title: ‘Long Ago Division: How to read historical maths historically’
    Abstract: Taking a close look at how long division was presented in the first widely-used English-language arithmetic textbook, we will discuss what it means to read historical mathematics historically. The Ground of Arts (1543) contained milestone expositions of what would become many standard set-pieces of basic mathematical education in the centuries to come, but beneath these familiar techniques there are important historical differences and lessons for us to examine. Considerations will include how to relate historical contexts to the mathematical text, how to identify and interpret information about authorship and audiences, how to use our technical knowledge of mathematics to understand past techniques, and how to identify where and how past assumptions and understandings differ from our own. The hands-on and interactive session will also involve grappling with the mundane but often difficult and significant details of reading very old texts: what letter is that? is that a misprint? what page are we on? We will detect a potential reference to a very old fart joke, which we will attempt to explain.
